# T01 — Quantum Mechanics Foundations

This document fixes the mathematical objects the simulator manipulates: states, operators,
composite systems, measurement, and density operators. Everything downstream (gates in T02,
noise in T04, numerics in T11) is expressed in the notation defined here.

## 1. Hilbert space and the postulates

**Postulate 1 (state).** An isolated physical system is associated with a complex Hilbert
space $\mathcal H$. The state of the system is a unit vector $|\psi\rangle \in \mathcal H$,
$\langle\psi|\psi\rangle = 1$. Vectors differing by a global phase $e^{i\alpha}$ describe the
same state.

**Postulate 2 (evolution).** A closed system evolves by a unitary operator,
$|\psi(t_2)\rangle = U(t_2,t_1)|\psi(t_1)\rangle$, generated by the Hamiltonian $H$ through the
Schrödinger equation

$$
i\hbar \frac{d}{dt}|\psi(t)\rangle = H(t)\,|\psi(t)\rangle,
\qquad
U(t,0) = \mathcal T \exp\!\left(-\frac{i}{\hbar}\int_0^t H(t')\,dt'\right).
\tag{1.1}
$$

For time-independent $H$, $U(t) = e^{-iHt/\hbar}$.

**Postulate 3 (measurement).** A measurement is described by a set of operators
$\{M_m\}$ on $\mathcal H$ with $\sum_m M_m^\dagger M_m = I$. Outcome $m$ occurs with
probability $p(m) = \langle\psi|M_m^\dagger M_m|\psi\rangle$ and leaves the system in
$M_m|\psi\rangle/\sqrt{p(m)}$.

**Postulate 4 (composition).** The state space of a composite system is the tensor product of
the component spaces, $\mathcal H = \mathcal H_{n-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal H_0$.

Dirac notation: a ket $|\psi\rangle$ is a column vector, the bra $\langle\psi| = |\psi\rangle^\dagger$
a row vector, $\langle\phi|\psi\rangle$ the inner product, $|\phi\rangle\langle\psi|$ an outer
product (rank-one operator). Operators are Hermitian ($A = A^\dagger$) for observables and
unitary ($U^\dagger U = I$) for evolution.

## 2. The qubit

A qubit is a system with $\mathcal H = \mathbb C^2$ and computational basis
$|0\rangle = (1,0)^T$, $|1\rangle = (0,1)^T$. A general state is

$$
|\psi\rangle = \alpha|0\rangle + \beta|1\rangle,\qquad |\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2 = 1,\quad \alpha,\beta\in\mathbb C.
\tag{2.1}
$$

Removing the global phase leaves two real parameters, the Bloch angles:

$$
|\psi\rangle = \cos\frac{\theta}{2}\,|0\rangle + e^{i\varphi}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\,|1\rangle,
\qquad 0\le\theta\le\pi,\; 0\le\varphi<2\pi.
\tag{2.2}
$$

The Bloch vector $\vec r = (\sin\theta\cos\varphi,\ \sin\theta\sin\varphi,\ \cos\theta)$ lies on the
unit sphere. $|0\rangle$ is the north pole ($\theta=0$), $|1\rangle$ the south pole.

Standard basis states on the axes:

| Label | State | $(\theta,\varphi)$ | $\vec r$ |
|-------|-------|--------------------|----------|
| $|0\rangle$ | $|0\rangle$ | $(0,\cdot)$ | $(0,0,1)$ |
| $|1\rangle$ | $|1\rangle$ | $(\pi,\cdot)$ | $(0,0,-1)$ |
| $|+\rangle$ | $(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$ | $(\pi/2,0)$ | $(1,0,0)$ |
| $|-\rangle$ | $(|0\rangle-|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$ | $(\pi/2,\pi)$ | $(-1,0,0)$ |
| $|{+i}\rangle$ | $(|0\rangle+i|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$ | $(\pi/2,\pi/2)$ | $(0,1,0)$ |
| $|{-i}\rangle$ | $(|0\rangle-i|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$ | $(\pi/2,3\pi/2)$ | $(0,-1,0)$ |

### 2.1 Pauli matrices

$$
I=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
Y=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\i&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
Z=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{2.3}
$$

Properties: Hermitian, unitary, traceless, $X^2=Y^2=Z^2=I$, and with
$\sigma_1=X,\sigma_2=Y,\sigma_3=Z$:

$$
[\sigma_j,\sigma_k] = 2i\,\epsilon_{jkl}\,\sigma_l,\qquad
\{\sigma_j,\sigma_k\} = 2\delta_{jk} I,\qquad
\sigma_j\sigma_k = \delta_{jk}I + i\,\epsilon_{jkl}\sigma_l.
\tag{2.4}
$$

Hence $XY = iZ$, $YZ = iX$, $ZX = iY$. The set $\{I,X,Y,Z\}$ is an orthogonal basis of $2\times2$
matrices under $\langle A,B\rangle = \mathrm{Tr}(A^\dagger B)/2$.

Ladder operators, with the convention that $\sigma^-$ lowers toward the ground state
$|0\rangle$:

$$
\sigma^\pm = \frac{X \mp iY}{2},\qquad
\sigma^- = \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix} = |0\rangle\langle1|,\qquad
\sigma^+ = \begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix} = |1\rangle\langle0|.
\tag{2.5}
$$

$\sigma^+\sigma^- = |1\rangle\langle1| = (I-Z)/2$ is the excited-state projector, the number
operator of the qubit. In this convention the qubit Hamiltonian is
$H = -\tfrac{1}{2}\hbar\omega_q Z$ so that $|1\rangle$ has energy $+\hbar\omega_q/2$ above
$|0\rangle$ by $\hbar\omega_q$.

The Bloch vector of a pure or mixed state is $r_j = \mathrm{Tr}(\rho\,\sigma_j)$ (§7).

## 3. Composite systems and little-endian indexing

For $n$ qubits, $\mathcal H = (\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes n}$ has dimension $2^n$. A computational
basis state is written with **qubit $n-1$ leftmost and qubit 0 rightmost**,

$$
|q_{n-1}\,q_{n-2}\,\ldots\,q_1\,q_0\rangle,\qquad \text{index } k = \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} q_j\,2^j .
\tag{3.1}
$$

The state vector is the Kronecker product in the same order:
$|\psi_{n-1}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|\psi_0\rangle$ has components
$\psi_k = \prod_j (\psi_j)_{q_j}$.

**Two qubits.** Index $k = 2q_1 + q_0$:

| index | ket $|q_1 q_0\rangle$ | $q_1$ | $q_0$ |
|------:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 0 | $|00\rangle$ | 0 | 0 |
| 1 | $|01\rangle$ | 0 | 1 |
| 2 | $|10\rangle$ | 1 | 0 |
| 3 | $|11\rangle$ | 1 | 1 |

Index 1 is "qubit 0 excited". A product state $(a|0\rangle+b|1\rangle)_{q_1}\otimes(c|0\rangle+d|1\rangle)_{q_0}$
has vector $(ac,\ ad,\ bc,\ bd)^T$.

**Three qubits.** Index $k = 4q_2 + 2q_1 + q_0$. The ket $|101\rangle$ means $q_2=1,q_1=0,q_0=1$,
index 5. The ket $|100\rangle$ is index 4 (only $q_2$ excited); $|001\rangle$ is index 1 (only
$q_0$ excited).

**Operators on selected qubits.** A single-qubit operator $A$ acting on qubit $j$ of an $n$-qubit
register is

$$
A^{(j)} = \underbrace{I\otimes\cdots\otimes I}_{n-1-j}\ \otimes\ A\ \otimes\ \underbrace{I\otimes\cdots\otimes I}_{j}.
\tag{3.2}
$$

So on two qubits $A^{(0)} = I\otimes A$ and $A^{(1)} = A\otimes I$. In components,
$(A^{(j)})_{k'k}$ is nonzero only when $k'$ and $k$ agree on all bits except bit $j$, and then
equals $A_{q'_j q_j}$. The simulator never forms (3.2); it applies $A$ to the pairs of amplitudes
$(\psi_k, \psi_{k\oplus 2^j})$ with $q_j(k)=0$ (T11 §1).

A Pauli string is written $P = P_{n-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes P_0$ and labelled by the string
`"P_{n-1}…P_0"`; the label `"ZI"` on two qubits is $Z^{(1)} = Z\otimes I$.

## 4. Entanglement

A pure state of a bipartite system $AB$ is a **product state** if $|\psi\rangle = |a\rangle\otimes|b\rangle$
for some $|a\rangle\in\mathcal H_A$, $|b\rangle\in\mathcal H_B$, and **entangled** otherwise. For two
qubits with $|\psi\rangle = \sum_{q_1q_0}c_{q_1q_0}|q_1q_0\rangle$, the state is a product state
iff the $2\times2$ coefficient matrix $C_{q_1q_0}$ has rank 1, i.e. $\det C = c_{00}c_{11}-c_{01}c_{10} = 0$.

**Bell states** (indices per (3.1)):

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\Phi^+\rangle &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\left(|00\rangle+|11\rangle\right) = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(1,0,0,1)^T,\\
|\Phi^-\rangle &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\left(|00\rangle-|11\rangle\right) = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(1,0,0,-1)^T,\\
|\Psi^+\rangle &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\left(|01\rangle+|10\rangle\right) = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(0,1,1,0)^T,\\
|\Psi^-\rangle &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\left(|01\rangle-|10\rangle\right) = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(0,1,-1,0)^T.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.1}
$$

They are orthonormal, and each is a $+1$ or $-1$ eigenstate of both $XX$ and $ZZ$:
$|\Phi^\pm\rangle$: $ZZ=+1$, $XX=\pm1$; $|\Psi^\pm\rangle$: $ZZ=-1$, $XX=\pm1$.
$|\Phi^+\rangle$ is produced by `h q[0]; cx q[0], q[1];` from $|00\rangle$.

**GHZ and W states** on $n$ qubits:

$$
|\mathrm{GHZ}_n\rangle = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\left(|0\cdots0\rangle + |1\cdots1\rangle\right),\qquad
|W_n\rangle = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}|2^j\rangle,
\tag{4.2}
$$

where $|2^j\rangle$ is the basis state with only qubit $j$ excited. Tracing out one qubit of
$|\mathrm{GHZ}\rangle$ leaves a separable mixture; tracing one qubit of $|W\rangle$ leaves an
entangled state. The two are not interconvertible by local operations.

## 5. Measurement

### 5.1 Projective measurement of an observable

A Hermitian observable $O = \sum_m \lambda_m \Pi_m$ with orthogonal projectors $\Pi_m$
(spectral decomposition). Measuring $O$ on $|\psi\rangle$:

$$
p(m) = \langle\psi|\Pi_m|\psi\rangle,\qquad
|\psi\rangle \to \frac{\Pi_m|\psi\rangle}{\sqrt{p(m)}},\qquad
\langle O\rangle = \sum_m \lambda_m p(m) = \langle\psi|O|\psi\rangle.
\tag{5.1}
$$

### 5.2 Computational-basis measurement of a register (Born rule)

Measuring all $n$ qubits in the $Z$ basis yields bit string $k$ with probability
$p(k) = |\psi_k|^2$, and the post-measurement state is $|k\rangle$.

### 5.3 Measuring a subset of qubits

Measuring the qubits in a set $S\subseteq\{0,\ldots,n-1\}$ and obtaining bits $\{b_j\}_{j\in S}$:

$$
p(b) = \sum_{k:\ q_j(k)=b_j\ \forall j\in S} |\psi_k|^2,
\qquad
\psi'_k = \begin{cases}\psi_k/\sqrt{p(b)} & q_j(k)=b_j\ \forall j\in S\\ 0 & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\tag{5.2}
$$

For a single qubit $j$, $p(1) = \sum_{k:\,q_j(k)=1}|\psi_k|^2$. This is the operation
`measure q[j] -> c[i]` in OpenQASM. The simulator computes $p(1)$ by a strided sum, draws the
outcome, zeroes the losing half, and rescales (T11 §2.5). Measuring qubits one at a time in any
order yields the same joint distribution as measuring them together, because $Z$ projectors on
different qubits commute.

### 5.4 Expectation values of Pauli strings

For $P = P_{n-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes P_0$ with $P_j\in\{I,X,Y,Z\}$,
$\langle P\rangle = \langle\psi|P|\psi\rangle \in [-1,1]$. Because $X = HZH$ and $Y = S H Z H S^\dagger$,
a hardware measurement of $\langle P\rangle$ applies the basis change to each non-identity
factor, measures in $Z$, and averages $(-1)^{\text{parity of measured bits on the support of } P}$
over shots. With $N$ shots the standard error is $\sqrt{(1-\langle P\rangle^2)/N}$.

### 5.5 Generalized measurements (POVM)

A POVM is a set $\{E_m\}$, $E_m\ge0$, $\sum_m E_m = I$, with $p(m) = \langle\psi|E_m|\psi\rangle$.
Every POVM on $\mathcal H$ can be realized as a projective measurement on $\mathcal H\otimes\mathcal H_{\rm anc}$
(Naimark). Readout of a superconducting qubit is a POVM whose elements are, to good
approximation, $E_0 = (1-\epsilon_{0})|0\rangle\langle0| + \epsilon_{1}|1\rangle\langle1|$,
$E_1 = I - E_0$, with $\epsilon_0,\epsilon_1$ the assignment error probabilities (T04 §9).

## 6. No-cloning and uncertainty

There is no unitary $U$ with $U|\psi\rangle|0\rangle = |\psi\rangle|\psi\rangle$ for all $|\psi\rangle$:
applying $U$ to two non-orthogonal states and taking inner products gives
$\langle\phi|\psi\rangle = \langle\phi|\psi\rangle^2$, which forces $\langle\phi|\psi\rangle\in\{0,1\}$.
Consequence for the lab: a state can be characterised only statistically (tomography, T10),
never by copying it.

Robertson uncertainty relation for observables $A,B$ with standard deviations $\Delta A,\Delta B$:

$$
\Delta A\,\Delta B \ \ge\ \tfrac12\left|\langle[A,B]\rangle\right|.
\tag{6.1}
$$

For $A=X$, $B=Y$ on a qubit: $\Delta X\,\Delta Y \ge |\langle Z\rangle|$.

## 7. Density operators

A **density operator** $\rho$ is positive semidefinite with $\mathrm{Tr}\rho = 1$. A pure state
has $\rho = |\psi\rangle\langle\psi|$; a statistical mixture $\{p_i, |\psi_i\rangle\}$ has
$\rho = \sum_i p_i|\psi_i\rangle\langle\psi_i|$. The decomposition is not unique, but $\rho$ is.

| Quantity | Formula | Pure | Maximally mixed |
|----------|---------|------|-----------------|
| Purity | $\mathrm{Tr}\rho^2$ | 1 | $1/d$ |
| Expectation | $\langle O\rangle = \mathrm{Tr}(\rho O)$ | | |
| Unitary evolution | $\rho\to U\rho U^\dagger$ | | |
| Measurement outcome $m$ | $p(m) = \mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_m\rho)$, $\rho\to\Pi_m\rho\Pi_m/p(m)$ | | |
| von Neumann equation | $\dot\rho = -\tfrac{i}{\hbar}[H,\rho]$ | | |

### 7.1 Single-qubit density operator and the Bloch ball

Every qubit density operator can be written

$$
\rho = \tfrac12\left(I + \vec r\cdot\vec\sigma\right) = \tfrac12\begin{pmatrix}1+r_z & r_x - i r_y\\ r_x + i r_y & 1-r_z\end{pmatrix},
\qquad r_j = \mathrm{Tr}(\rho\sigma_j),\qquad |\vec r|\le1.
\tag{7.1}
$$

$|\vec r| = 1$ iff $\rho$ is pure; $\mathrm{Tr}\rho^2 = (1+|\vec r|^2)/2$. The maximally mixed state
$I/2$ sits at the origin. The `viz` Bloch sphere draws $\vec r$ from (7.1) for any qubit of any
backend that exposes a reduced density operator.

### 7.2 Partial trace

For $\rho$ on $\mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_B$ (with $A$ the higher-order factor, $d_A d_B$ total
dimension, composite index $k = a\,d_B + b$):

$$
(\rho_A)_{a a'} = \sum_{b=0}^{d_B-1} \rho_{(a d_B + b),\,(a' d_B + b)},
\qquad
(\rho_B)_{b b'} = \sum_{a=0}^{d_A-1} \rho_{(a d_B + b),\,(a d_B + b')}.
\tag{7.2}
$$

For an $n$-qubit register, tracing out a set $T$ of qubits to keep the set $K$:

$$
(\rho_K)_{k k'} = \sum_{t\in\{0,1\}^{|T|}} \rho_{\,\mathrm{merge}(k,t),\ \mathrm{merge}(k',t)},
\tag{7.3}
$$

where $\mathrm{merge}(k,t)$ interleaves the bits of $k$ (at positions $K$) and $t$ (at positions
$T$) into a full $n$-bit index. This is the operation behind every "click a qubit on the chip to
see its Bloch vector" interaction; it costs $O(2^{n})$ from a state vector via
$\rho_K = \sum_t |\psi_t\rangle\langle\psi_t|$ with $|\psi_t\rangle$ the sub-vector at fixed $t$.

**Example.** For $|\Phi^+\rangle$, $\rho = \tfrac12(|00\rangle\langle00| + |00\rangle\langle11| + |11\rangle\langle00| + |11\rangle\langle11|)$
and tracing out either qubit gives $\rho_{q_0} = \rho_{q_1} = I/2$: each qubit alone is
maximally mixed, Bloch vector $\vec 0$, though the pair is pure.

### 7.3 Schmidt decomposition

Any pure bipartite state can be written

$$
|\psi\rangle = \sum_{i=1}^{r}\sqrt{\lambda_i}\,|u_i\rangle_A\otimes|v_i\rangle_B,
\qquad \lambda_i>0,\ \sum_i\lambda_i = 1,
\tag{7.4}
$$

with orthonormal $\{|u_i\rangle\}$, $\{|v_i\rangle\}$. Computation: reshape the amplitude vector into
the $d_A\times d_B$ matrix $C_{ab} = \psi_{a d_B + b}$; its singular value decomposition
$C = U\Sigma V^\dagger$ gives $\sqrt{\lambda_i} = \Sigma_{ii}$, $|u_i\rangle$ = columns of $U$,
$|v_i\rangle$ = columns of $V$ (conjugated rows of $V^\dagger$). The Schmidt rank $r$ is 1 iff the
state is a product state. The reduced states are $\rho_A = \sum_i\lambda_i|u_i\rangle\langle u_i|$,
$\rho_B = \sum_i\lambda_i|v_i\rangle\langle v_i|$, with the same nonzero spectrum.

## 8. Entropies and distances

**von Neumann entropy** (in bits):

$$
S(\rho) = -\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\log_2\rho) = -\sum_i \lambda_i\log_2\lambda_i,
\tag{8.1}
$$

with $\lambda_i$ the eigenvalues of $\rho$; $S = 0$ for pure states, $S = \log_2 d$ for $I/d$.

**Entanglement entropy** of a bipartition $A|B$ of a pure state: $E = S(\rho_A) = S(\rho_B)$,
equal to the Shannon entropy of the Schmidt coefficients. For a Bell state $E = 1$ bit; for
$|\mathrm{GHZ}_n\rangle$ any bipartition has $E = 1$; for $|W_n\rangle$ a single qubit against the
rest has $E = h_2(1/n)$ with $h_2(p) = -p\log_2 p - (1-p)\log_2(1-p)$.

**Mutual information** $I(A{:}B) = S(\rho_A) + S(\rho_B) - S(\rho_{AB})$, nonzero for classical
or quantum correlations.

**Concurrence** of a two-qubit state $\rho$ (Wootters):

$$
C(\rho) = \max\left(0,\ \mu_1-\mu_2-\mu_3-\mu_4\right),\qquad
\tilde\rho = (Y\otimes Y)\,\rho^*\,(Y\otimes Y),
\tag{8.2}
$$

where $\mu_1\ge\mu_2\ge\mu_3\ge\mu_4$ are the square roots of the eigenvalues of $\rho\tilde\rho$
and $\rho^*$ is the complex conjugate in the computational basis. $C = 0$ iff separable,
$C = 1$ for Bell states. For a pure state $C = 2|\det C_{q_1q_0}| = 2|c_{00}c_{11}-c_{01}c_{10}|$.

**Fidelity** between states, in the squared convention used throughout this project:

$$
F(\rho,\sigma) = \left(\mathrm{Tr}\sqrt{\sqrt\rho\,\sigma\sqrt\rho}\right)^2,
\qquad
F(|\psi\rangle,\sigma) = \langle\psi|\sigma|\psi\rangle,
\qquad
F(|\psi\rangle,|\phi\rangle) = |\langle\psi|\phi\rangle|^2.
\tag{8.3}
$$

$0\le F\le1$, $F=1$ iff $\rho=\sigma$. (Nielsen–Chuang define the unsquared quantity; all
QuantumXLab fidelities, including gate fidelities in T10, are squared so that they read as
probabilities.)

**Trace distance** $D(\rho,\sigma) = \tfrac12\mathrm{Tr}|\rho-\sigma| = \tfrac12\sum_i|\eta_i|$
with $\eta_i$ the eigenvalues of $\rho-\sigma$; it bounds the distinguishability of the two
states in any measurement, and $1-\sqrt F \le D \le \sqrt{1-F}$. For single qubits
$D = |\vec r_\rho - \vec r_\sigma|/2$.

**Distances between outcome distributions** $p,q$ over bit strings (used to compare backends
and to compare shots with the exact distribution):

$$
F_{\rm c}(p,q) = \Big(\sum_k\sqrt{p_k q_k}\Big)^2,\qquad
H^2(p,q) = 1-\sum_k\sqrt{p_k q_k},\qquad
\mathrm{TVD}(p,q) = \tfrac12\sum_k|p_k-q_k|.
\tag{8.4}
$$

$H$ is the Hellinger distance, $F_{\rm c}$ the classical fidelity; $H^2 = 1-\sqrt{F_{\rm c}}$.

## 9. Worked example: three-qubit state inspection

Take $|\psi\rangle = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(|000\rangle + |011\rangle + |101\rangle + |110\rangle\right)$,
indices $\{0,3,5,6\}$ — the even-parity states with $q_0 = q_1\oplus q_2$.

- Measuring $q_0$: $p(1) = |\psi_1|^2+|\psi_3|^2+|\psi_5|^2+|\psi_7|^2 = 0 + \tfrac14 + \tfrac14 + 0 = \tfrac12$.
- $\langle Z_2 Z_1 Z_0\rangle = \sum_k |\psi_k|^2 (-1)^{\mathrm{popcount}(k)} = +1$ (every term has
  even parity).
- Reduced state of $q_0$: $\rho_{q_0} = I/2$ (each of $q_0=0,1$ carries probability $1/2$ with
  orthogonal partners), $E(q_0 | q_1q_2) = 1$ bit.
- Reduced state of the pair $(q_1,q_0)$ after tracing $q_2$: the $q_2=0$ block is
  $\tfrac12(|00\rangle+|11\rangle)$ and the $q_2=1$ block is $\tfrac12(|01\rangle+|10\rangle)$, so
  $\rho_{q_1q_0} = \tfrac12|\Phi^+\rangle\langle\Phi^+| + \tfrac12|\Psi^+\rangle\langle\Psi^+| = \tfrac14(I + X\otimes X)$.
  This is the separable mixture $\tfrac12(|{+}{+}\rangle\langle{+}{+}| + |{-}{-}\rangle\langle{-}{-}|)$: concurrence
  $C = 0$, yet $I(q_1{:}q_0) = 1 + 1 - 1 = 1$ bit. The pair is classically correlated (equal
  $X$ outcomes) without being entangled — the distinction the entanglement graph of spec 21
  must draw (edge weight from $C$, not from mutual information).
